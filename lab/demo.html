VoqalizeMediaManager — loopback lab

There is no server here. This page opens two RTCPeerConnections inside this one browser tab and wires them to each other — the offer and answer are exchanged in JavaScript, the ICE candidates are handed straight across, and nothing leaves the machine. The "remote" video below is your own camera after it has been encoded, sent over RTP, and decoded again.

PyGato is not involved and no signalling happens. What this proves is the half that belongs to the browser: the manager acquires devices, publishes clones, the peer connection encodes them, and a device switch reaches the wire through replaceTrack without renegotiating. If a number below is moving, that part of the pipeline is real.

1 · Call

Start acquires the mic, publishes a clone, opens the loopback and applies the encoding policy. Everything after that happens on the live call: no button below re-negotiates.

3 · Microphone level, measured at the receiving end

This is RTCInboundRtpStreamStats.audioLevel from the receiving peer — the level of audio that actually survived encode, transmission and decode. It is deliberately not a Web Audio AnalyserNode on the capture track: SPEC decision 7 keeps Web Audio out of the capture path, and a meter built there would be measuring a pipeline we do not ship.

4 · Per-lane encoder, live (1 Hz)

Lane (m-line) Frames encoded FPS Resolution Bitrate Packets sent Decoded / received Codec Limited by

Healthy looks like this. audio (m-line 0): packets sent climbing by ~50/s, bytes climbing, audioLevel above 0 when you speak — there is no framesEncoded for audio, and a blank there is correct. video (m-line 1): frames encoded climbing steadily, ~24–30 fps, 640×360, roughly 300–600 kbps — the policy is maintain-framerate, so a face stays smooth and goes soft under pressure. screenVideo (m-line 2): frames encoded climbing slowly — about 5 fps is the policy, not a fault — at your real display resolution, up to 1.5 Mbps. It is maintain-resolution: a scroll should drop frames, never sharpness. A lane that is idle shows a dash. A lane that is live and stuck at 0 frames is the failure worth reporting.

5 · What the manager thinks

m-lines stay at 3, always. All three transceivers are created sendonly before the first offer, because supportsScreenShare is a constant true (decision 4). That is why starting a screen share mid-call shows renegotiations needed: 0 — the lane was already there.

6 · Event log

Every manager callback, every replaceTrack the hook performed, every device error, and the browser's own "Stop sharing".

7 · Things to try

  1. Unplug a USB headset mid-call. Watch the log for onAvailableMicsUpdated and the re-acquire. The frames-encoded counter on the video lane must not reset, and the audio packet counter must keep climbing — the mic was swapped with replaceTrack, not by rebuilding the call.
  2. Click the browser's own "Stop sharing" chrome while a screen share is live (not the button on this page). The log should show onScreenTrackStopped, the screen lane should go idle, and m-lines must still read 3 with renegotiations still 0.
  3. Switch the Bluetooth output in the speaker dropdown while playing the remote audio. Firefox does not implement setSinkId; if the dropdown is empty or the selection does nothing, that is the engine, and the log says which.
  4. Background this tab for ten seconds, then come back. Frame counters keep climbing on some engines and throttle hard on others — either is fine, but the call must still be connected and the counters must resume, not restart.
  5. Deny a permission. Reload, block the camera, and press Camera. It should land as one typed onDeviceError in the log with the browser's own exception name, and the microphone must keep working — one denial must not cost the other device.
  6. Turn the camera off and on again while a screen share is running. The screen lane's counters must not so much as blink: two lanes, two encoders (decision 10).
